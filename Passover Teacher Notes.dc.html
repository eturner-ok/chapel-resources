Big Idea

On the night everything changed, God kept a promise he had made to Abraham centuries earlier, to the very day (12:41). Death came for the firstborn of Egypt, the nation that had drowned Israel's sons. In every Israelite house a lamb died instead, and by the next day the families who trusted God's word and marked their doors were walking out of Egypt free (Num 33:3).

If A Student Asks

Two hard questions

Tap a question to open it

Don't rush past it. This night is a showdown over whose sons matter. Pharaoh had Hebrew baby boys thrown into the Nile (1:22). Before Moses went back to Egypt, God said Israel is "my firstborn son" and that Pharaoh's firstborn would pay for Pharaoh's refusal (4:22–23). "Firstborn" meant the oldest son at any age, so many were grown men.

You can say

"This is one of the hardest parts of the Bible, and it's supposed to feel heavy. Pharaoh had been killing Hebrew baby boys, and God stood up for those families. We don't have to pretend it's easy."

No. In earlier plagues, he protected Israel with no marker at all (8:22–23; 9:26; 10:23). He calls the blood "a sign for you" (12:13). Marking the doorframe was a family's way of saying they believed him.

You can say

"God didn't need a map. The blood was their way of saying, 'We trust you.'"
Heads Up

A student who has lost a brother or sister may hear this story differently. If someone goes quiet, check in privately later. And don't let "Egypt deserved it" become the takeaway. Chapters 11 and 12 don't celebrate these deaths. They record a great cry (12:30).

Connecting the Dots

For the student who is…

Dressed for a trip they hadn't taken yet.

Israel ate this meal belted, in sandals, staff in hand (12:11), while they were still slaves. Nothing had changed yet, and they got ready anyway because God said they were leaving. That's what faith looked like that night: obeying before seeing. When a student is stuck waiting on God about something, this is the picture to hand them.

God guards the door.

A lot of anxiety is fear that something bad will get in. Exodus 12:23 answers with God at the door: the LORD "will pass over the door and will not allow the destroyer to enter your houses." Isaiah uses the same verb for God shielding Jerusalem like a bird over its nest (Isa 31:5), and Exodus calls this "a night of watching by the LORD" (12:42).

Fun Fact
2,000+ years

This past April, on Mount Gerizim near Nablus, the Samaritans slaughtered Passover lambs at twilight, following Exodus 12. Fewer than 1,000 Samaritans remain, and they've kept the sacrifice going for more than two thousand years.
