# Teacher Notes · The Passover (Exodus 11–12)

Nov 6, 2026 · @Eric Turner

## One Story Timeline

Creation · Fall · Flood · Abraham · Isaac and the Ram · Jacob · Joseph · The Burning Bush · **Passover (Nov 18)** · Through the Water (Dec 2)

## Where We Are

Everything in the story so far has been heading toward this night. God promised Abraham a nation and a land, and he warned him that his descendants would be slaves in a foreign land before walking out with great possessions (Gen 15:13–14). On the mountain, Isaac asked, "Where is the lamb?" (Gen 22:7). Joseph brought Jacob's family of seventy down to Egypt, and Genesis ends with his coffin there and a promise that God would bring them out (Gen 50:24–26). Then came centuries of slavery and silence, until God heard their groaning and remembered his covenant (Exod 2:24). Now, after nine plagues, Pharaoh still says no, and God announces one more (11:1). On this night, the promise to Abraham finally comes true by the mighty hand of God (3:19; 13:9), and every Israelite house has a lamb.

## Big Idea

**On the night everything changed, God kept a promise he had made to Abraham centuries earlier, to the very day (12:41). Death came for the firstborn of Egypt, the nation that had drowned Israel's sons. In every Israelite house a lamb died instead, and by the next day the families who trusted God's word and marked their doors were walking out of Egypt free (Num 33:3).**

## If A Student Asks

**"Why would God kill kids who didn't do anything?"**

Don't rush past it. This night is a showdown over whose sons matter. Pharaoh had Hebrew baby boys thrown into the Nile (1:22). Before Moses went back to Egypt, God said Israel is "my firstborn son" and that Pharaoh's firstborn would pay for Pharaoh's refusal (4:22–23). "Firstborn" meant the oldest son at any age, so many were grown men.

*You can say:* "This is one of the hardest parts of the Bible, and it's supposed to feel heavy. Pharaoh had been killing Hebrew baby boys, and God stood up for those families. We don't have to pretend it's easy."

**"Did God need the blood to find Israel's houses?"**

No. In earlier plagues, he protected Israel with no marker at all (8:22–23; 9:26; 10:23). He calls the blood "a sign for you" (12:13). Marking the doorframe was a family's way of saying they believed him.

*You can say:* "God didn't need a map. The blood was their way of saying, 'We trust you.'"

## Heads Up

A student who has lost a brother or sister may hear this story differently. If someone goes quiet, check in privately later. And don't let "Egypt deserved it" become the takeaway. Chapters 11 and 12 don't celebrate these deaths. They record a great cry (12:30).

## Fun Fact

This past April, on Mount Gerizim near Nablus, the Samaritans slaughtered Passover lambs at twilight, following Exodus 12. Fewer than 1,000 Samaritans remain, and they've kept the sacrifice going for more than two thousand years.

## Connecting the Dots

**Dressed for a trip they hadn't taken yet.** Israel ate this meal belted, in sandals, staff in hand (12:11), while they were still slaves. Nothing had changed yet, and they got ready anyway because God said they were leaving. That's what faith looked like that night: obeying before seeing. When a student is stuck waiting on God about something, this is the picture to hand them.

**God guards the door.** A lot of anxiety is fear that something bad will get in. Exodus 12:23 answers with God at the door: the LORD "will pass over the door and will not allow the destroyer to enter your houses." Isaiah uses the same verb for God shielding Jerusalem like a bird over its nest (Isa 31:5), and Exodus calls this "a night of watching by the LORD" (12:42).

## Key Terms

- **Pesach (PAY-sakh):** Hebrew for Passover. Traditionally "pass over," though many scholars read it as "protect" (Isa 31:5).
- **Matzah (MAHT-zuh):** bread made without yeast. Israel left before the dough could rise (12:34, 39).
- **Hyssop:** a small, common leafy plant used as a brush for the blood (12:22).

## Document Notes

- The Big Idea's "in every Israelite house a lamb died instead" follows the substitution reading (Motyer; 1 Cor 5:7). Exodus 12:30 itself describes Egyptian houses.
- God gives Moses the firstborn warning in 4:22–23. The text first records it spoken to Pharaoh in 11:4–8.
- What *pesach* means is debated: "pass over," "protect," and "have compassion" all have serious support. The Go Deeper lays them out.
